# Translation glossary and style rules

Working glossary for the Claude-drafted Brazilian Portuguese (`pt-BR`) and Haitian Creole (`ht`) translations. Both varieties are **working assumptions**, not a decision by Dr. Bourjeily or the PI (see `docs/translation/README.md`). Every term here is a draft for a qualified medical translator to confirm; nothing in this file is approved.

## Style rules

| Rule | Brazilian Portuguese (`pt-BR`) | Haitian Creole (`ht`) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Address the learner | `você` throughout; never `tu`; Brazilian verb forms | `ou` throughout |
| Learner is pregnant | Feminine agreement for the learner (`grávida`, `cansada`) where English addresses her | Creole has no grammatical gender; no change needed |
| Register | Plain patient-education language, short sentences | Plain spoken Kreyòl (standard orthography of the 1979/1980 Haitian spelling), not French; do not use French words where a common Kreyòl word exists |
| Brand and device names | Keep `PAP`, `CPAP`, `APAP`, `BiPAP`, product and drug brand names unchanged | Same |
| Numbers, units, URLs, phone numbers | Unchanged; decimal comma only where English uses a decimal in prose (`1,5`) | Unchanged (keep decimal point) |
| Associations vs effects | Keep "linked to" / "associated with" as association (`está associado a`, `está ligado a`); never turn into "causes" | `gen rapò ak`, `asosye ak`; never turn into `lakòz` (causes) unless English says cause |
| Hedges | Keep "may", "can", "might", "some", "about" (`pode`, `alguns`, `cerca de`) | `ka`, `kèk`, `anviwon` |
| Negations and warnings | Keep every "not", "never", "do not", "only" | Keep every `pa`, `pa janm`, `sèlman` |
| PAP vs oxygen | PAP = air pressure (`pressão de ar`), never `oxigênio` | PAP = `presyon lè`, never `oksijèn` |
