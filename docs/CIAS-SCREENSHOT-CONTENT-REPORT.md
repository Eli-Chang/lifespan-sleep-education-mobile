In this video, we'll dive more into understanding why OSA, or obstructive sleep apnea, happens.

In OSA, your throat relaxes so much that it narrows and/or creates a blockage during sleep, leading to difficulty breathing. (Imagine sucking air through a straw and then pinching the straw.) ### Screen 6 — Establishing a Good Sleep Routine Part 1: Bedtime Routine

Source screenshot: `screenshots/Screenshot 2026-07-29 171851.png`  
Confidence: HIGH

#### Source text

Establishing a good sleep routine is one of the most important lifestyle modifications that can improve your sleep, mood, and daily energy. There are three components of a good sleep routine that we'll cover.

The first component of a good sleep routine is establishing a relaxing bedtime routine about an hour before going to bed.

This can involve light (relatively boring) reading, stretching, or taking a warm bath. Listening to calming music and dimming the lights are good practices too. Simply having a bedtime routine signals to your brain and body that it's time to wind down, leading to reduced stress and anxiety, and improve sleep quality, and even cognitive function.

### Screen 7 — Establishing a Good Sleep Routine Part 2: Sleep/Wake Times

Source screenshot: `screenshots/Screenshot 2026-07-29 171855.png`  
Confidence: HIGH

#### Source text

The second component of a good sleep routine is keeping the same bedtime and wake times.

Wake up around the same time each morning and go to sleep around the same time at night—even if you didn't sleep well the night before. This anchors your body's internal clock (circadian rhythm), and can help you be more tired at night (especially if you didn't sleep well the night before).

This applies especially for the weekends! It may be tempting to sleep in on the weekends, but when you shift your sleep schedule back and forth on the weekends (called social jet lag), it's similar to your body crossing time zones on a weekly basis. If you find yourself tired during the week, it may be a sign that you need better sleep in general.

### Screen 8 — Establishing a Good Sleep Routine Part 3: Avoiding Blue Light Exposure Close to Bedtime

Source screenshot: `screenshots/Screenshot 2026-07-29 171900.png`  
Confidence: HIGH

#### Source text

The third component of a good sleep routine is avoiding blue light before bedtime.

Blue lights (and bright lights in general) inhibit your body's production of the sleepy chemical (melatonin), which is great in the morning when you want to wake up, but is detrimental at night when you want to feel tired. Most electronic devices these days emit lots of blue light, which is why scrolling on your phone before bed is very bad for your sleep.

Instead, here are some changes to your routine that can help you feel more tired at night and improve sleep quality:

• Keep the lighting at home to a minimum before bedtime. Dim the lights around the house, and when light is still needed, switch to warmer hues (orange/red).
• Use night shift/color filters on your phone and reduce the brightness. The best solution would be to not use your devices close to bedtime. Night shift can make your phone emit less blue light at night or turning on a red color filter can completely eliminate blue light emission from your phone.

### Screen 10 — Addressing Difficulty When Falling Asleep

Source screenshot: `screenshots/Screenshot 2026-07-29 171914.png`  
Confidence: HIGH

#### Source text

What should you do if you have trouble falling asleep?

Having trouble falling asleep may happen around pregnancy. If having trouble falling asleep last for more than 20 minutes, the best thing you can do is to get out of bed, go to another room, and engage in a quiet and relaxing activity in low-light conditions, such as reading, listening to calming music, or practicing meditation. You would want to associate the bed with sleep; so getting out of the bedroom when you are not feeling sleepy helps maintain that association. Only return to bed when you feel sleepy.

What you should NOT do if you're having trouble falling asleep is using electronic devices. Electronic devices can signal to your brain to stay awake for longer.

Do NOT engage in stimulating activities, like reading a thriller or listening to loud music.

Do NOT turn on bright lights in the middle of the night, as that can mess up your ability to fall back to sleep.

Do NOT check the time, as this can increase anxiety and frustration around sleep.

### Screen 16 — Establishing a Good Sleep Environment

The third component of an optimal sleep environment is to keep your bedroom as completely dark as possible.

### Screen 21 — Chrononutrition (Meal Timing for Sleep)

Source screenshot: `screenshots/Screenshot 2026-07-29 172018.png`  
Confidence: HIGH

#### Source text

Chrononutrition refers to how meal timing can affect your sleep and circadian rhythm. Let's look at some of the best chrononutrition advice.

Front-load your calories and hydration earlier in the day. “Eat breakfast like a king, lunch like a prince, and dinner like a peasant.”

• Your body processes food best earlier in the day (the food is more likely to be used for fuel rather than fat).
• Your body also feels fuller from the same meal if eaten earlier in the day due to better hormonal signaling and metabolism. This means that eating a big breakfast, then shifting lunch and dinner earlier in the day, could help you feel fuller, with fewer cravings at night.
• Consider eating lunch and dinner earlier in the day, finishing your last meal 4–5 hours before bedtime for sleep and general health.
• Early hydration helps prevent you from having to pee at night, which pregnant women are more susceptible to since the baby can compress the bladder, and because overall blood volume is higher.

Try to avoid big meals at least 3–4 hours before bed.

• This helps prevent your body from having to actively digest food while trying to sleep at the same time, which can worsen sleep quality.
• Arguably, finishing your last meal even farther from bedtime (5+ hours) is even better, since your resting heart rate will drop, which is a very strong indicator of sleep quality.

#### Source text

Background

Nasal congestion from pregnancy rhinitis (nasal inflammation) is very common, with some reviews reporting it in up to 42% of pregnancies. It is also common for nasal congestion to get worse later in pregnancy.

Causes and Complications

Body–fluid changes and pregnancy hormones like estrogen and progesterone can all cause the lining of the nose to swell, which can make breathing through the nose harder and cause women to feel congested. This nasal congestion can disrupt sleep, and as the nose is part of the airway, nasal congestion can exacerbate snoring, and worsen sleep apnea. Addressing nasal congestion may help breathing during sleep.

Nasal congestion can also impact how well people tolerate the CPAP mask. As the CPAP device blows air through the mask (whether it is a nasal mask, a full face mask, or nasal pillows), nasal congestion often makes CPAP more uncomfortable and may increase leak through the mouth. However, this can be addressed to improve comfort.

### Screen 4 — Gastroesophageal Reflux Disease (GERD)

Source screenshot: `screenshots/Screenshot 2026-07-29 172146.png`  
Confidence: HIGH

#### Source text

Background

Symptoms of GERD (such as heartburn) during pregnancy are very common, with studies reporting a prevalence of 30–80%. This condition also often worsens later in pregnancy.

Causes and Complications

Pregnancy can relax the muscle tone in the lower esophageal sphincter (the muscle that normally keeps stomach contents in the stomach). This lower tone can increase the likelihood of stomach contents, including the acid produced by the stomach, going back up the esophagus. This can cause common symptoms such as heartburn, regurgitation, a sour taste in the mouth, or nausea.

OSA and GERD can both individually worsen sleep quality. In addition, OSA may worsen GERD, as when you try to inhale with a blocked airway, a suction-like negative pressure can pull stomach contents upward into the esophagus.

### Screen 6 — Gastroesophageal Reflux Disease (GERD) Treatment

Source screenshot: `screenshots/Screenshot 2026-07-29 172156.png`  
Confidence: HIGH

#### Source text

The best first-line treatments for GERD and heartburn are usually dietary changes and bed adjustments, such as:

• smaller meals with reduced fat content of meals
• less caffeine (for example coffee, tea, coke or pepsi, energy drinks.)
• Finishing your last meal 3–4 hours before bedtime
• Consider avoiding common trigger foods, such as fatty fried foods, spicy foods, tomato, citrus, or carbonated drinks.
