## Module 5: Healthy Sleep Hygiene (app `m5` and `m5b`)

### m5-q5, Check 1: The Pitfalls of Poor Sleep (frames 183, 188, 190). Key: depression, chronic disease, fatigue.
- Immune system strengthened: "Poor sleep will not strengthen your immune system. However, poor sleep does weaken the immune system, as it decreases the production of the beneficial immune system molecules."
- Poor sleep isn't that bad: "Poor sleep, unfortunately, has many consequences that can affect many aspects of your physical and mental health."
- You will be shorter: "Luckily, for full-grown adults, poor sleep will not make you shorter. However, for individuals still going through puberty, poor sleep may blunt growth, as it is the time when your body releases the most growth hormone."
- Chronic disease: "Poor sleep (and sleep deprivation) are consistently linked to physiological changes that increase risk of diabetes, high blood pressure, high cholesterol, obesity, and more."
- Depression and mood: "Poor sleep disrupts the production and sensitivity of key mood regulators that can keep you in a positive mood, and keeps stress hormones chronically high."
- Daytime fatigue: "Poor sleep is unable to fully clear the chemical adenosine (which helps make you sleepy), which causes daytime fatigue even after sleep. In addition, poor sleep disrupts other bodily functions that provide energy and fuel for your body."

### m5-q6, Check 2: Poor Sleep During Pregnancy (frames 195, 200, 205). Key: C-section, longer labors, preterm birth.
- Eye color: "Sleep does not have an impact on the genetic coding of physical traits such as eye color."
- Nocturnal baby: "We do not know yet if a baby's sleep habits are affected by mom's own sleep schedule."
- Labor will stop: "Fortunately, poor sleep will not stop labor. However, labor has been show to be longer in women who sleep less than 6 hours."
- Preterm birth: "Poor sleep is thought to increase chronic, systemic inflammation, which may signal for early contractions."
- Longer labor: "Studies have shown that pregnant women who get less than 6 hours of sleep often have significantly longer labors."
- C-section: "It is not entirely clear how and why that happens... Poor sleep may decrease pain tolerance and energy storage for contractions, which can lead to 'failure to progress'."

### m5-q7, Check 3: Sleep Hygiene Components (frames 213, 214, 226). Key: exercise, nutritious meals, sunshine.
- Caffeine before bed: "This is a very bad idea. Caffeine inhibits your ability to feel tired. Not only will you have a harder time falling asleep, but the amount of deep sleep you get will also suffer."

- Long nap: "...this will greatly inhibit your ability to fall asleep again when bedtime comes, which will impair your sleep and throw off your circadian rhythm."
- Sunshine: "Daily sunshine triggers a healthy spike in cortisol and delays sleepy chemical (melatonin) production until bedtime. This properly sets your circadian rhythm."
- Nutritious meals: "A nutritious diet prevents your body from being in an inflammatory state. This will facilitate better sleep at night and restfulness."
- Exercise: "Exercise uses more of the body's energy currency ATP, which creates more of the biproduct adenosine (the chemical that makes you sleepy). This makes you feel more tired at night!"

### m5-q3, Check 8: Caffeine (frames 285, 286, 301). Key: individual metabolism, slower late-pregnancy processing, 200 mg.
